Frameworks chosen: RTF, ICIO, Chain-of-Thought (CoT)

1) RTF (Role, Task, Format)
   Found at: frompting.com/frameworks
   Why chosen: RTF is the most minimal structured framework available (role,
   task, output format only, no dedicated context/examples section). It was
   chosen as a lightweight baseline to see how much a short, direct prompt
   can achieve on a schema-grounded generation task when the schema is
   compressed into a single short paragraph.
   What is expected: Fastest inference time of the three (shortest prompt,
   shortest expected output: just the SQL). Accuracy expected to be
   reasonable on simple, single-table questions, but weaker on questions that
   require joining across RESTAURANT / LOCATION / GEOGRAPHIC or handling the
   lowercase text data, since RTF leaves little room to call out such
   pitfalls explicitly.

2) ICIO (Input, Context, Instruction, Output)
   Found at: frompting.com/frameworks
   Why chosen: ICIO was chosen because it cleanly separates the part of the
   prompt that changes every call (Input = the question) from the stable
   schema/domain knowledge (Context), the behavioral rules (Instruction), and
   the strict response contract (Output). This separation mirrors how a real
   NL2SQL pipeline would template its prompts, and it gives an explicit place
   to document known pitfalls of this dataset (values stored in lowercase,
   addresses living in LOCATION rather than RESTAURANT, which tables must be
   joined and on what keys).
   What is expected: Higher accuracy than RTF on join-heavy and
   case-sensitivity-related questions, because the Context/Instruction
   sections make these rules explicit rather than implicit. Expected cost:
   slightly longer prompt and marginally slower inference than RTF, but
   still a single-shot (non-reasoning) response.

3) Chain-of-Thought (CoT)
   Found at: frompting.com/frameworks (also a standard prompting technique
   in the prompt-engineering literature, e.g. Wei et al., 2022)
   Why chosen: CoT was chosen to directly test the effect of explicit
   intermediate reasoning on this task. Many questions in the dataset are
   compositional (e.g., "best place for X food in city Y" implicitly
   requires identifying the filter columns, the join needed for city/food
   type, and an ORDER BY + LIMIT for "best"). Forcing the model to decompose
   the question into entities, required joins, filters, and
   ordering/limiting logic before writing the query was expected to reduce
   reasoning errors in exactly these cases.
   What is expected: Potentially the highest raw accuracy of the three,
   at the cost of being the slowest and most token-heavy prompt
   to run, since the model must produce reasoning text in addition to the
   final query. This makes CoT the most interesting comparison point for
   the accuracy-vs-inference-time tradeoff required by Task 2.

Summary of expected contrast across the three prompts:
   RTF  -> fastest, simplest, likely weakest on hard/join/case-sensitivity
           cases (lower bound baseline).
   ICIO -> same single-shot cost profile as RTF but with explicit schema and
           data-quirk documentation, expected to recover most of the
           accuracy RTF loses on harder questions.
   CoT  -> slowest and most verbose, expected to recover the most accuracy on
           compositional/superlative questions via explicit stepwise
           reasoning, directly testing the reasoning-vs-latency tradeoff.
